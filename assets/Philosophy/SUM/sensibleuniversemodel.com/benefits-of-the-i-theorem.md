 **Definition of a Fact Witness** 



A fact witness is a person with knowledge about the relevant facts in a particular case who appears before an arbitral tribunal, through a witness statement and potentially in person, to provide testimony pertaining to the facts.






 **Who may be a witness** 






Any person with information of the relevant facts in the arbitration based on his or her own direct knowledge, the statements of others, or review of documents, can be a witness, including party representatives and interested persons. However, the arbitral tribunal has discretion to determine the weight to be given to the witness’ testimony.


The term  **"witness" in a spiritual context** Itgenerally refers to one of two distinct concepts, depending on the tradition: the  **"inner witness" (or witnessing consciousness)** , a neutral observer of one's own internal experiences, or the act of  **"bearing witness"**  to one's faith or a divine experience. 




 **The Inner Witness / Witnessing Consciousness (Eastern Traditions, Mindfulness)** 



This concept, often found in Advaita Vedanta, Yoga, and mindfulness practices, describes the capacity of pure awareness (known as  _sakshi_  in Sanskrit) to observe thoughts, emotions, and sensations without identification or judgment. 



-  **Detached Observation:**  It involves stepping back to watch the mind's activities, such as perceptions, feelings, memories, and imaginations, as if watching a movie or a parade, without getting caught up in them.

-  **Disidentification:**  The primary goal is to realize that one is the unchangeable, observing awareness, not the fleeting contents of the mind or ego. This process is crucial for spiritual liberation or self-realization, as identifying with the mind's "fluctuations" is seen as the cause of suffering.

-  **Stillness and Peace:**  Cultivating the inner witness leads to a sense of inner calm and detachment ( _vairagya_ ), even amidst worldly activities. This stillness allows for a higher perspective and the realization of one's true nature as pure, unchanging consciousness.

-  **Practice:**  This state is often cultivated through meditation and concentration, where one learns to let distracting thoughts come and go without feeding them energy or pursuing them. 






 **Bearing Witness (Western Traditions, Christianity)** 



In Christianity and other Western religious contexts, "witness" has a different, more relational and active meaning. 



-  **Sharing Personal Experience:**  It involves sharing one's personal experience, testimony, and the transformative power of faith (e.g., in Jesus Christ) with others.

-  **Evidence of Faith:**  A Christian's life and actions are meant to serve as evidence, or a "witness," of their faith and the reality of God's work in the world. This is seen less as a command to proselytize and more as a natural outpouring of the indwelling Holy Spirit.

-  **Relational Act:**  Witnessing can also be a compassionate, non-judgmental relational act of being fully present for another person, especially in times of grief or suffering, allowing them to feel heard and seen. 



In summary, the spiritual meaning of "witness" can be an  **internal, detached state of pure awareness**  or an  **external, relational act of testifying spiritually, and consciously.**
