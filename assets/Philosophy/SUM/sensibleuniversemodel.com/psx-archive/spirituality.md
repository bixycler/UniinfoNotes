### Abstract


Panpsychism holds that consciousness is fundamental and ubiquitous in matter. This paper argues against that view on logical, explanatory, and empirical grounds. We address the recurring attempt to tie consciousness to global physical parameters—especially the cosmological constant Λ—and show why this move fails. Λ regulates expansion; it does not license attributions of mentality. ## 1) What panpsychism claims—and why it is attractive


Panpsychism (micropsychism, cosmopsychism, or “ubiquitous proto-mentality”) promises:

- A direct answer to the “hard problem” (why experience exists at all) by declaring experience primitive.

- A unified ontology (no ontological “gap” between mind and matter).

- A way to fit “qualia” into physics without reduction.


 **Why it is attractive** : it eliminates explanatory gaps by fiat. But this comes at a cost: explanatory inflation (postulating mental properties everywhere) without new predictions or mechanistic detail.





## 2) Steelmanning panpsychism before we critique it


 **Best version**  (to be fair):

-  **Constitutive micropsychism** : fundamental particles have very simple phenomenal properties; complex consciousness results from combinations.

-  **Cosmopsychism** : the universe has a single, fundamental consciousness; individual minds are aspects/partitions of that whole.

-  **Neutral monism/dual-aspect** : there is one neutral stuff with both physical and phenomenal aspects; science accesses structural/causal, not intrinsic/qualitative features.


These versions try to dodge “spookiness” by aligning with physics’ structural realism. Still, each runs into serious problems.





## 3) Core problems for panpsychism

### (A) The combination problem (and its mirror)


If micro-entities are little mindlets,  **how do they combine**  into one unified subject (a single point-of-view) rather than a mere aggregate? Physical composition has clear laws; mental “fusion” does not. Cosmopsychism flips the issue and then faces the  **decombination**  (or subject-splitting) problem: how does one cosmic mind become many distinct subjects like you and me?


 **Status** : no worked mechanism that (i) is consistent with physics, (ii) preserves the unity of phenomenal fields, and (iii) explains the clear individuation boundaries of organisms and brains.

### (B) Explanatory idleness


Attributing “protomental” properties to quarks explains no new data about particle behavior, chemistry, or neural dynamics. Physics works without mental variables at the micro-level. Panpsychism  **adds entities without necessity** . Occam bites hard.

### (C) Causal redundancy / epiphenomenal drift


If physical laws are closed and complete at micro-scales, where does “proto-experience” do work? If it does none, it is epiphenomenal. If it does, it must alter physical predictions—yet no deviations are observed.

### (D) Mismatch of structure


Consciousness exhibits  **integrated, temporally deep, content-rich**  structure (binding, access, report). Elementary particles have spin, charge, mass— **simple**  state-spaces. The structural gap is massive; calling both “experience” obscures rather than clarifies.

### (E) Boundary and subject individuation problems


If everything has some consciousness,  **what counts as a subject** ? A crystal? A river? A galaxy? Panpsychism supplies no principled cutoff. Ethics and agency become unintelligible if all aggregates qualify equally.

### (F) Testability deficit


Panpsychism  **predicts nothing new**  and  **rules out nothing**  we can measure. Any alleged micro-experience fits any dataset. That is a red flag for a scientific theory.





## 4) Λ is not a consciousness operator


 **Λ** , in Einstein’s equations, acts as vacuum energy density driving accelerated expansion. It:

- Enters gravitation as a uniform term,

- Constrains large-scale dynamics,

- Has  **no**  degrees of freedom that resemble representational, integrated, or valenced states.


Attaching “mind” to Λ  **confuses global dynamics with phenomenology** . Λ shapes spacetime curvature;  **consciousness**  involves information integration, access, and metacognitive control in specific  **nonlinear, recurrent** architectures (brains or brain-like systems). Nothing in Λ implies the presence of subjects, qualia, or perspective.


Within ΨΞ/SUM: Λ can serve as a  **normative baseline for coherence**  (a “Love” constant in your vocabulary), but  **coherence ≠ consciousness** . A laser is coherent; it is not conscious. Global curvature may scaffold order, not experience.

 **Core Arguments Against Panpsychism** 



 **1. The Combination Problem: Insoluble Aggregation of Minds** 

The combination problem, first articulated by William James in 1890 and formalized by William Seager in 1995, poses the most formidable challenge to constitutive panpsychism (where macro-consciousness arises from micro-conscious elements). If every fundamental particle possesses rudimentary "proto-consciousness," how do these disparate micro-minds fuse into the unified, coherent macro-consciousness of a human brain? Unlike physical composition (e.g., quarks forming protons via strong force), there is no analogous mechanism for mental summation. James likened it to "mind-dust," arguing that trillions of atomic experiences cannot spontaneously cohere into a singular subjectivity without arbitrary assumptions.

Contemporary elaborations highlight subtypes: the "subject-summing problem" (how multiple subjects merge into one) and the "phenomenal bonding problem" (what relations bind micro-experiences?). Philip Goff attempts a "phenomenal bonding" solution, positing fundamental laws where micro-phenomena bond via causal or spatial relations, but this remains vague and untestable, akin to introducing ad-hoc epicycles. Luke Roelofs (2019) defends constitutive micropsychism by analogizing to social groups, but critics note this fails for non-overlapping subjectivities—e.g., why don't rocks exhibit unified consciousness despite particle composition?

Empirically, neuroscience shows consciousness correlating with integrated neural networks (e.g., via gamma oscillations ~30-120 Hz), not particle-level minds. Panpsychism's inability to specify bonding thresholds renders it incoherent, violating explanatory closure.

 **2. Explanatory Idleness and Lack of Predictions** 

Panpsychism posits universal consciousness but renders it causally inert, failing to influence physical laws or predict phenomena. Particle physics (e.g., Standard Model) fully describes electron behavior via quantum electrodynamics without mental properties; adding proto-consciousness explains nothing new, breaching Occam's razor. As Bernardo Kastrup critiques, it introduces ontological bloat without pragmatic value, unlike emergent physicalism, which aligns with evidence from lesion studies (e.g., split-brain patients showing divided consciousness).

No testable hypotheses emerge: How to detect proto-consciousness in quarks? Panpsychism's vagueness contrasts with SUM's Fr-H-F, where observable resonances (e.g., galactic ridges) predict patterns without mental primitives.

 **3. The Flipped Zombie Argument:**  Undermining Panpsychism's Foundations

The classic zombie argument (Chalmers, 1996) posits beings physically identical to humans but lacking consciousness, challenging physicalism by suggesting conceivability implies possibility. However, physicist Sean Carroll flips this: If zombies are conceivable, panpsychism fares worse, as it implies consciousness is epiphenomenal—present in all matter yet behaviorally irrelevant. In a panpsychist universe, particles' proto-minds exert no causal influence, mirroring zombies but universally.

This inversion yields:

 **- No Explanatory Power:**  Consciousness becomes a passive witness, unable to affect evolution or behavior.

 **- Incompatibility with Physics:**  Electrons lack complexity for "memory" or "choice," per quantum mechanics.

 **- Untestability:**  No empirical distinction from a non-conscious world.

 **- Non-Parsimony:** Adds mental ontology without necessity.

 **- Scientific Incoherence:**  Contradicts neuroscience's causal links (e.g., qualia influencing reports).

Physicalism resolves this via weak emergence: Consciousness arises from information integration, testable via models like Integrated Information Theory (IIT).

 **4. No Empirical Evidence or Testability** 

Panpsychism lacks detectable signs of consciousness in inanimate matter. Analogies to human minds fail for extrema (e.g., atoms vs. brains), and emergence from non-conscious substrates is conceivable, per functionalist accounts. It risks "neuro-solipsism," dissolving external reality into perceptions, undermining science. Critics like Anil Seth argue it's unscientific, ignoring neural correlates.

 **5. Issues with Intermediate Subjects and Moral Implications** 

Defining conscious boundaries is arbitrary: Are viruses or AI "conscious"? This leads to a "moral parody," questioning ethics if all matter suffers. Cosmopsychism (universe as one mind) swaps combination for "decombination" problems—how one mind fragments into individuals.
